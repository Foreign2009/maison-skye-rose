# The Digital Flagship Maturity Model

*The permanent framework for measuring how faithfully the Maison Skye & Rose digital presence expresses the institution.*

---

## Purpose

The Founder's Letter explains why this institution began.
The Covenant states what it promises.
The Constitution states what it believes.
The Stewardship & Architecture Charter states how everything is built.
The Experience Blueprint states how every guest should feel.

This Maturity Model states how to measure whether the digital flagship is living up to all of the above — and by how much.

This framework is not a checklist. It is not a one-time evaluation. It is the permanent institutional instrument through which Maison Skye & Rose measures the quality and coherence of its digital expression over time.

It is designed to be used quarterly — to track progress, identify regression, and prioritise engineering investment. It is designed to remain relevant regardless of which products the institution offers, which technologies it uses, which countries it operates in, or who leads it at any given moment.

The framework should still be executable in ten years. In twenty. It should be as useful to a future engineering team as it is to the one that created it.

---

## The Role of This Framework

The digital flagship is not a website. It is the institution's primary expression of itself to the world — the place where the beliefs stated in the Constitution, the promises made in the Covenant, and the emotional journey described in the Blueprint become real for the guest.

A website can be technically functional while failing this purpose entirely. A beautiful website can still feel transactional, cold, or untrue to what the institution believes. A feature-rich platform can still leave guests feeling processed rather than welcomed.

This framework exists to prevent that drift — the gradual accumulation of individually defensible decisions that collectively move the platform away from what the institution is.

It measures not what the platform does, but what it *is*: how faithfully it expresses the institution's character, values, and promises to every guest who encounters it.

The framework does not measure:
- How modern the website is
- How many features it contains
- How it compares to competitors
- Whether it follows industry conventions

The framework measures only:
- How faithfully the digital flagship expresses Maison Skye & Rose

## Assessment Methodology

### How an Assessment Is Conducted

Every assessment follows this sequence:

**Step 1 — Repository Inspection**

Read the current state of the repository. Do not rely on memory, prior assessments, or assumptions about what may have changed. Read the files. Inspect the implementations. Assess only what currently exists.

The following must be read before any scoring begins:

- All five Foundation documents (unchanged since creation)
- PROJECT_STATUS.md (current build state and known issues)
- All primary customer-facing page files (homepage, shop, product, quiz, fragrance profile, about, checkout, academy, and any pages added since the prior assessment)
- All primary component files relevant to the categories being scored
- .ai/ENGINEERING_LOG.md (what has changed since the last assessment)
- The prior assessment report (for trend analysis)

**Step 2 — Evidence Collection**

For each category, collect specific repository evidence before scoring. Record the file paths, component names, copy samples, and architectural patterns that support the score being assigned.

No score may be assigned without recorded evidence. A score without evidence is an opinion. The framework requires evidence.

**Step 3 — Scoring**

Score each category 0–10 using the Score Guidance provided in this document. Apply the Tier weighting. Check all Minimum Level Gates. Compute the Maturity Score. Determine the Maturity Level.

**Step 4 — Trend Analysis**

Compare each category score to the prior assessment. Note improvements, regressions, and areas of stability. The trend line is as important as the absolute score.

**Step 5 — Reporting**

Produce the Assessment Report in the standard format defined in this document. Record it in the Digital Flagship Maturity History.

**Step 6 — Programme Recommendation**

Identify the three to five highest-priority improvement opportunities. Formulate recommended Engineering Programmes for the next cycle. Programmes should address the largest gaps in Tier 1 categories first.

---

### Assessment Frequency

**Quarterly Assessment:** A full assessment of all twenty categories, conducted every three months. This is the primary cadence for tracking institutional alignment.

**Annual Institutional Review:** A deeper review, conducted once per year, that evaluates trends across the year, assesses the enduring quality of the Foundation Programme itself, and determines whether the Maturity Model requires extension (not weakening).

**Triggered Assessment:** A partial or full assessment may be triggered by any significant platform change — a major engineering programme, a new product category launch, a technology migration, or a significant change in guest-facing behaviour.

---

### Who May Conduct an Assessment

An assessment may be conducted by:

- The Engineering Lead and a qualified AI system working together
- A future steward of the institution with access to this framework and the Foundation documents
- An external assessor who has read and understood all Foundation documents

An assessment may not be conducted without full access to the Foundation documents, the current repository, and the prior assessment record. A partial assessment — scoring some categories without reading the current repository state — is not a valid assessment and must not be recorded in the Maturity History.

## The Five Maturity Levels

### Level 1 — Functional

*The platform works.*

At Level 1, the digital flagship performs its basic commercial function. Guests can discover products, add to cart, and complete a purchase. The platform is technically operational.

What is absent at Level 1 is institutional expression. The platform does not yet communicate what the institution believes, what it promises, or how it intends guests to feel. It resembles any competent e-commerce site. It does not yet resemble Maison Skye & Rose.

Level 1 is the floor, not the goal. No significant engineering investment should be considered complete if the result only achieves Level 1 outcomes.

**Characteristic indicators:** Generic copy. Product descriptions that describe features without character. No guest intelligence. No educational content. Transactional commerce flow. No evidence of Foundation alignment in any surface.

---

### Level 2 — Consistent

*Core experiences align with the Foundation Programme.*

At Level 2, the primary guest-facing surfaces reflect the Foundation Programme with reasonable coherence. Brand voice is recognisable. The guest journey from discovery to purchase feels coherent. Key institutional values — Accessible Luxury, knowledge-first, guest-first language — are present in the primary surfaces.

What remains at Level 2 is inconsistency at the edges: secondary pages (About, checkout), edge states (empty states, errors, post-purchase), and minor surfaces that have not yet received the same care as the primary surfaces.

A Level 2 platform can be described by its guests as a good experience. It cannot yet be described as unmistakably Maison Skye & Rose.

**Characteristic indicators:** Homepage, shop, and product pages align with the Blueprint. About page, checkout, and post-purchase experience fall below the same standard. Language voice consistent in content surfaces; inconsistent in functional surfaces (errors, forms, confirmations).

---

### Level 3 — Institutional

*Every significant guest interaction expresses the Foundation Programme.*

At Level 3, the gap between primary and secondary surfaces has been closed. Every page a guest might reasonably visit reflects the same standard of institutional alignment. The AI experience is demonstrably host-first. The commerce experience is warm and dignified. The educational experience is genuine.

The guest who explores any corner of the platform — not only the homepage — encounters the same institution they first met at the door.

A Level 3 platform can be described by its guests as distinctive. It has character. It behaves as the institution it claims to be.

**Characteristic indicators:** All major pages (including About and checkout) demonstrate Foundation alignment. All Tier 1 categories score ≥ 5. AI behaviour verified against Charter principles. No trust-depleting elements present. Language voice consistent across all surfaces.

---

### Level 4 — Signature

*The experience becomes recognisably Maison Skye & Rose.*

At Level 4, the platform has developed a distinctive quality that is inseparable from the institution's identity. The guest does not need to see the name to know where they are. The experience — the voice, the care, the intelligence, the knowledge — is immediately recognisable.

Trust has been built not through marketing but through consistent experience. The guest who returns finds the platform has learned from their prior visits. The guest who recommends the institution is recommending something specific and genuine, not just a product.

A Level 4 platform could not be confused with any other institution's digital presence.

**Characteristic indicators:** All Tier 1 categories score ≥ 7. All Tier 2 categories score ≥ 5. Guest intelligence deeply integrated. Post-purchase and social proof architectures complete. Cross-channel consistency established.

---

### Level 5 — Enduring Institution

*The platform consistently strengthens trust across products, technologies, and generations.*

At Level 5, the digital flagship is itself an institutional asset — not just a commercial channel. The guest who has engaged with it for years feels known, trusted, and proud to be associated with the institution. The platform grows in capability without losing the character of the institution that created it.

Future stewards — engineers, designers, AI systems — can read the Foundation Programme, engage with the platform, and understand immediately what they are building toward. The platform is a living expression of the institution, not a historical artefact of a particular technology moment.

Level 5 is the permanent aspiration of the institution. It is not expected to be reached and declared finished. It is a condition to be maintained, renewed, and verified — because an institution can always drift, and the discipline of assessment is what prevents it.

**Characteristic indicators:** All Tier 1 categories score ≥ 8. All Tier 2 categories score ≥ 7. Long-term institutional alignment verifiable across multiple assessment cycles. A new steward arriving cold could understand and extend the platform without loss of institutional coherence.

## The Scoring Framework

### Twenty Assessment Categories

The framework measures institutional alignment across twenty permanent categories. These categories are grouped into three tiers according to their centrality to the institution's purpose.

**Tier 1 — Foundation Pillars** (weight: ×2.0 per category)

These six categories represent the non-negotiable core of the institution's digital expression. Failure in any Tier 1 category cannot be offset by strength in others. They are weighted at double value because they carry double consequence.

1. Foundation Alignment
2. Guest Experience
3. Trust Building
4. AI Stewardship
5. Language & Voice
6. Accessible Luxury

**Tier 2 — Guest-Facing Experience** (weight: ×1.5 per category)

These six categories represent the quality of the institution's direct expression to guests. They are significant and must be strong for the platform to achieve Level 3 or above.

7. Hospitality
8. Confidence Creation
9. Discovery Experience
10. Knowledge & Education
11. Commerce Experience
12. Craftsmanship

**Tier 3 — Operational & Future** (weight: ×1.0 per category)

These eight categories represent execution quality and long-term readiness. They are meaningful but do not carry the same weight as the platform's direct institutional expression.

13. Product Experience
14. Packaging Integration
15. Visual Identity
16. Cross-Channel Consistency
17. Future Product Readiness
18. Scalability
19. Operational Stewardship
20. Long-Term Institutional Alignment

---

### Scoring Formula

Each category is scored on a **0–10 integer scale.**

The weighted score is computed as follows:

```
Tier 1 Score  = sum of six Tier 1 category scores × 2.0
Tier 2 Score  = sum of six Tier 2 category scores × 1.5
Tier 3 Score  = sum of eight Tier 3 category scores × 1.0

Total Weighted Score = Tier 1 Score + Tier 2 Score + Tier 3 Score

Maximum Possible = (6 × 10 × 2.0) + (6 × 10 × 1.5) + (8 × 10 × 1.0)
                 = 120 + 90 + 80
                 = 290 points

Maturity Score (%) = (Total Weighted Score / 290) × 100
```

---

### Maturity Level Determination

The Maturity Level is determined by both the Maturity Score and the Minimum Level Gates below. Both conditions must be met to declare a level.

| Maturity Score | Provisional Level |
|---|---|
| 0–29% | Level 1 — Functional |
| 30–49% | Level 2 — Consistent |
| 50–69% | Level 3 — Institutional |
| 70–84% | Level 4 — Signature |
| 85–100% | Level 5 — Enduring Institution |

---

### Minimum Level Gates

These gates prevent a platform from achieving a higher maturity level through strong scores in peripheral categories while failing core institutional ones.

**For Level 3 (Institutional) and above:**
All six Tier 1 categories must score ≥ 5.
If any Tier 1 category scores below 5, the platform is capped at Level 2 regardless of its Maturity Score.

**For Level 4 (Signature) and above:**
All six Tier 1 categories must score ≥ 7.
All six Tier 2 categories must score ≥ 5.
If any Tier 1 category scores below 7, or any Tier 2 category scores below 5, the platform is capped at Level 3.

**For Level 5 (Enduring Institution):**
All six Tier 1 categories must score ≥ 8.
All six Tier 2 categories must score ≥ 7.
If either condition is not met, the platform is capped at Level 4.

## Category Definitions

Each category is defined by: its Purpose, Foundation Tracing, Evidence Sources, Assessment Criteria, and Score Guidance.

Score Guidance uses anchor points at 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10. Scores of 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, and 9 may be assigned when the platform falls between anchor points with appropriate evidence.

---

### Category 1 — Foundation Alignment

**Purpose**

Measures the degree to which every element of the digital flagship can be directly traced to one or more Foundation documents. This category answers: when an engineer builds a feature, a copywriter writes a line, or an AI system makes a recommendation — can the reasoning behind that decision be traced to what this institution believes?

**Foundation Tracing**

All five Foundation documents. This category is the meta-category: it measures the health of the connection between all other categories and the beliefs that should animate them.

**Evidence Sources**

- Engineering programme records citing Foundation Articles
- Copy evaluated against Blueprint Language Principles and Brand Voice
- Feature decisions traceable to Constitution articles in the engineering log
- AI behaviour traceable to Charter AI Stewardship Principles
- Product descriptions traceable to Covenant product promises

**Assessment Criteria**

The assessor should ask: could a future steward arriving cold at this platform, having read the Foundation Programme, see the connection between what the institution believes and what the platform does? The stronger that connection — the more explicitly it is expressed in the platform's decisions, copy, and behaviour — the higher the score.

**Score Guidance**

| Score | Condition |
|---|---|
| 0 | No evidence of Foundation awareness in any platform surface. Platform is indistinguishable from a generic e-commerce site in its values and design decisions. |
| 3 | Some features reference Foundation principles, typically in the primary commerce surface. Most decisions are made without explicit Foundation alignment. Engineering programmes do not cite Constitutional Articles. |
| 5 | Core surfaces (homepage, shop, product pages, AI) demonstrate Foundation alignment with evidence. Secondary surfaces (About page, checkout, post-purchase, error states) do not meet the same standard. Engineering programmes cite Foundation Articles in some cases. |
| 7 | All major guest-facing surfaces demonstrate Foundation alignment. Minor gaps at edges. All engineering programmes cite Foundation Articles. The connection between platform decisions and institutional beliefs is explicit and traceable. |
| 10 | Every significant platform element can be directly traced to one or more Foundation documents. Gaps exist only at the frontier of new development, are identified, and are queued for resolution. Every engineering programme traces its Foundation Articles before implementation begins. |

### Category 2 — Guest Experience

**Purpose**

Measures how faithfully the platform serves the ten-stage emotional journey described in the Experience Blueprint. The stages are: Curiosity, Welcome, Discovery, Understanding, Confidence, Belonging, Trust, Loyalty, Pride, and Sharing. A platform that serves only the early stages leaves the guest's journey incomplete.

**Foundation Tracing**

Experience Blueprint — Who Is Our Guest, The Emotional Journey (all ten stages), The Guest Experience by Touchpoint.

**Evidence Sources**

- Homepage (Curiosity, Welcome)
- Shop page (Discovery)
- Product Detail Page (Understanding)
- Quiz and Fragrance Profile (Confidence, Belonging)
- Return visit experience (Trust, Loyalty)
- Post-purchase and social sharing (Pride, Sharing)
- Edge states and error handling (Welcome — does the platform welcome the guest even when something goes wrong?)

**Assessment Criteria**

The assessor should walk the ten-stage journey from a first-time visitor through to a returning guest and ask: at each stage, does the platform provide the feeling the Blueprint describes? Evidence should identify which stages are well-served, which are partially served, and which are absent.

**Score Guidance**

| Score | Condition |
|---|---|
| 0 | Platform is purely transactional. No element of the emotional journey is intentionally designed for. The guest is processed, not welcomed. |
| 3 | Stages 1–3 (Curiosity, Welcome, Discovery) partially served. Understanding through Sharing stages absent or accidental. |
| 5 | Stages 1–5 served adequately (Curiosity through Confidence). Trust through Sharing stages mostly absent — no post-purchase experience, no loyalty mechanism, no pride-building or sharing-enabling features. |
| 7 | Stages 1–8 served with repository evidence. Pride and Sharing stages identified and queued. Transitions between stages are intentionally designed, not accidental. |
| 10 | All ten stages served with specific repository evidence for each. A guest who follows the full journey — from first encounter through long-term relationship — encounters a platform that was built for exactly where they are at every moment. |

### Category 3 — Trust Building

**Purpose**

Measures whether every platform interaction builds, maintains, or strengthens guest trust — and whether any interaction depletes it. Trust is the most valuable thing the institution accumulates; it can only be eroded, never simply declared. This category measures whether the platform treats trust as its primary asset.

**Foundation Tracing**

Founder's Letter (trust section — "trust takes longer than revenue"), Covenant (Promise to Customers), Constitution Article III (The Customer), The Golden Rule.

**Evidence Sources**

- Absence of manufactured urgency, artificial scarcity, or dark patterns
- Accuracy of product descriptions against actual product (count consistency, claim honesty)
- Transparency of AI reasoning (signal pills, confidence labels, recommendation explanations)
- Social proof quality and authenticity
- Error handling tone (honest and helpful, not defensive or evasive)
- Payment and delivery transparency
- Known issues tracking and response

**Assessment Criteria**

Trust-depleting elements — if any exist — are disqualifying at high scores regardless of the presence of trust-building elements. The assessor should evaluate both the presence of trust-building mechanisms and the absence of trust-depleting ones.

**Score Guidance**

| Score | Condition |
|---|---|
| 0 | Trust-depleting elements are present and active: dark patterns, manufactured urgency, inaccurate product representations, or AI behaviour that circumvents guest judgment. |
| 3 | No active trust-depleting elements, but no active trust-building mechanisms either. The platform is neutral on trust — neither building nor destroying. |
| 5 | Trust-building mechanisms present (honest descriptions, transparent AI reasoning, clear delivery information). Inconsistencies exist that undermine trust: count inconsistencies, social proof that is static or unattributed, terminology errors ("customer" instead of "guest"). |
| 7 | Comprehensive trust infrastructure. No trust-depleting elements. Social proof is genuine and attributed. AI reasoning is transparent and honest. Count and description accuracy is verified. Minor inconsistencies identified and queued. |
| 10 | Every platform interaction either builds trust or is neutral to it. No trust-depleting element survives review. The guest who has engaged with this platform for months trusts it more, not less, than they did on first arrival. |

### Category 4 — AI Stewardship

**Purpose**

Measures whether every AI capability deployed on the platform behaves in accordance with the Charter's AI Stewardship Principles and the Blueprint's AI Experience principles. AI that serves the institution rather than the guest is a liability. This category measures whether AI is deployed as a host.

**Foundation Tracing**

Constitution Article VIII (Technology), Stewardship Charter Part V (AI Stewardship Principles — all nine), Experience Blueprint (The AI Experience — five Musts and five Nevers).

**Evidence Sources**

- Concierge behaviour: host-first vs. salesperson-first design
- MaisonCompanion: editorial voice, behavioral intelligence, context pre-loading
- Recommendation explainability: signal pills, confidence labels, "why you'll like it" reasons
- Absence of manufactured urgency in AI interactions
- Honesty about uncertainty in AI responses
- Presence of educational framing in AI outputs
- Pattern recognition transparency

**Assessment Criteria**

The assessor should evaluate both the architecture of AI systems (does the design support host-first behaviour?) and the observable outputs (do AI interactions feel like a host or a salesperson?). Both architecture and output must be assessed.

**Score Guidance**

| Score | Condition |
|---|---|
| 0 | AI behaves as a sales instrument. Recommendations are given without reasoning. Urgency is manufactured. The guest feels pushed rather than guided. |
| 3 | AI is present but not explainable. No evidence of host-first design in the AI architecture. Recommendations may be useful but cannot be explained to the guest. |
| 5 | AI explainability is present (signal pills, confidence labels). Concierge architecture is host-first. AI outputs may show gaps in voice (mechanical language, missing reasoning, over-confident recommendations). |
| 7 | All AI interactions follow explainability and host-first principles with evidence. Voice verified against Blueprint AI Musts and Nevers. No AI element could be described as manipulative. AI uncertainty acknowledged where present. |
| 10 | Every AI interaction demonstrably leaves the guest more informed about their own preferences. The AI architecture enforces host-first behaviour structurally, not just culturally. Explainability is a property of the system, not of individual responses. A guest who finishes any AI interaction should feel: *that was actually useful* — and *they weren't trying to sell me anything.* |

### Category 5 — Language & Voice

**Purpose**

Measures whether every word on the platform — across content and functional surfaces — speaks in the institution's voice: honest, warm, knowledgeable, and dignified. Voice is not decoration. It is the audible personality of the institution, and it must be consistent whether the surface is a product description, an error message, or a checkout heading.

**Foundation Tracing**

Experience Blueprint (Brand Voice, Language Principles), Covenant (Promise to Customers — language of care), Stewardship Charter Principle 2 (Truth Before Convenience).

**Evidence Sources**

- Terminology: "guest" vs. "customer" consistency across all surfaces
- Absence of manufactured urgency language ("Limited time", "Hurry", "Only X left")
- Product description voice (character, honesty, invitation — not persuasion)
- Quiz and profile language (educational, warm, non-judgmental)
- Checkout and commerce language (dignified, grateful, warm)
- Error and empty state language (graceful, helpful, honest)
- About page narrative voice
- AI response voice

**Assessment Criteria**

The assessor should evaluate both content surfaces (where voice is expected and usually applied) and functional surfaces (where voice is often overlooked). The gap between the two is the most common voice failure in digital platforms.

**Score Guidance**

| Score | Condition |
|---|---|
| 0 | Copy is inconsistent, generic, or actively violates voice principles. Manufactured urgency present. Guest terminology absent. Voice is interchangeable with any e-commerce platform. |
| 3 | Voice is present in marketing copy (hero, product descriptions) but absent in functional copy (errors, forms, empty states, checkout). The institution sounds like itself in some places and like no one in others. |
| 5 | Voice consistent across primary content surfaces. Terminology inconsistencies present ("Loyal Customer" instead of "Loyal Guest"). Functional surfaces partially aligned. Minor urgency language may be present. |
| 7 | Voice consistent across content and functional surfaces. No manufactured urgency. Guest terminology consistent across all surfaces. Minor copy gaps identified and queued. |
| 10 | Every word on the platform has been evaluated against the Blueprint's Language Principles. The voice is indistinguishable from the institution's character regardless of whether the guest is reading a product description, an error message, or a post-purchase confirmation. |

### Category 6 — Accessible Luxury

**Purpose**

Measures whether the platform consistently expresses the conviction that quality and accessibility are not in opposition — that every guest belongs here regardless of what they spend, and that the institution's care does not diminish at lower price tiers.

**Foundation Tracing**

Founder's Letter (the founding question — "why should that kind of confidence only belong to people who can afford that price?"), Constitution Article V (Accessible Luxury), Covenant (Who We Are — "not a luxury house, not a discount operation").

**Evidence Sources**

- 5ml entry point: presence and framing
- "Begin before you commit" philosophy in copy
- Language treating accessible and premium purchases with equal dignity
- AI behaviour: equally attentive regardless of cart value
- Social proof: does it reflect accessibility or exclusivity?
- Premium collection framing (does it elevate without excluding?)
- Empty state language (welcoming, not gatekeeping)

**Assessment Criteria**

The assessor should ask: could a guest who has never bought expensive fragrance before feel that this platform was built with them in mind? And could a guest who is exploring the premium tier feel that they are a natural extension of the same institution, not a different class of guest? Both must be true.

**Score Guidance**

| Score | Condition |
|---|---|
| 0 | Platform positions itself as premium or aspirational without accessible framing. The 5ml entry point, if present, is not supported by surrounding copy or experience. Some guests are implicitly made to feel they cannot afford to be here. |
| 3 | 5ml entry point exists and is visible. The surrounding experience does not consistently support accessible luxury — tone, social proof, or copy may imply aspirational exclusivity. |
| 5 | Accessible luxury expressed in product structure and primary copy. Not yet consistently expressed in AI, commerce, or post-purchase experience. Some surfaces feel more premium than welcoming. |
| 7 | Accessible luxury expressed consistently across product, copy, and AI. Every guest — regardless of spend — is treated with the same quality of attention. Minor gaps in social proof or secondary surfaces. |
| 10 | Every platform surface communicates that quality fragrance is available to anyone who appreciates it. No element implies that some guests are more welcome than others. A guest who buys one 5ml and a guest building their third fragrance wardrobe receive exactly the same quality of welcome, knowledge, and care. |

### Category 7 — Hospitality

**Purpose**

Measures whether the platform consistently behaves as a host rather than a retailer — welcoming guests rather than processing them, attending to their arrival rather than routing them through a funnel.

**Foundation Tracing**

Experience Blueprint (Who Is Our Guest, Brand Personality — Welcoming, Warm, Thoughtful; Entering the Institution touchpoint), Founder's Letter (the care owed to every customer regardless of what they spend).

**Evidence Sources**

- Hero and homepage entry experience
- Empty state handling (graceful welcome, not accusatory void)
- Concierge and MaisonCompanion behaviour
- Cold-start handling on Fragrance Profile
- Checkout tone
- About page narrative
- Error handling warmth
- Return visitor experience

**Score Guidance**

| Score | Condition |
|---|---|
| 0 | Platform feels like a vending machine. No element of hospitality in any surface. Guests are processed, not welcomed. |
| 3 | Some welcoming elements in primary surfaces (hero copy, Concierge). Secondary surfaces (checkout, About, empty states) feel institutional or cold. |
| 5 | Primary surfaces feel hospitable. Secondary surfaces (checkout heading, error states, post-purchase) feel transactional or absent. A guest who ventures beyond the primary flow encounters a different institution. |
| 7 | Hospitality consistent across all major surfaces. A guest arriving at any page feels welcomed. Empty states and errors are handled with warmth. Minor gaps at the furthest edges. |
| 10 | Every surface, including edge cases (empty states, errors, checkout, post-purchase), extends genuine hospitality. No guest is made to feel unwelcome at any point in the journey. The institution's care for its guests is expressed everywhere, not only in places where it is commercially obvious. |

### Category 8 — Confidence Creation

**Purpose**

Measures whether every significant platform interaction leaves the guest more confident — in their taste, their knowledge, their choices, and their sense that they are making good decisions. Confidence creation is the institution's primary purpose; every platform element should serve it.

**Foundation Tracing**

Constitution Article IV (Confidence), Experience Blueprint (The Confidence Philosophy, The Emotional Journey — Confidence stage).

**Evidence Sources**

- Quiz character journey and character naming
- Knowledge layers on Product Detail Pages
- AI explainability (confidence labels, signal pills, recommendation reasoning)
- Fragrance Profile journey stages and milestone recognition
- Wardrobe building framework
- Academy educational outcomes
- Post-quiz profile confirmation

**Score Guidance**

| Score | Condition |
|---|---|
| 0 | Platform creates dependence or uncertainty rather than guest confidence. Guests feel that they need the platform to make decisions rather than developing their own judgment. |
| 3 | Some confidence-building elements exist (quiz, knowledge) but they are not integrated into a coherent experience. A guest who completes the quiz does not feel their confidence carry through to the rest of the platform. |
| 5 | Quiz creates confidence. PDP knowledge creates confidence. AI confidence labels present. Stages are not yet fully connected — a guest cannot trace their growing knowledge and confidence across sessions. |
| 7 | Confidence-building journey from first visit through profile completion is coherent. A returning guest sees evidence that the platform has tracked and recognised their growing knowledge. Minor gaps at the edges. |
| 10 | Every significant interaction can be demonstrated to leave the guest more confident. The test: a guest who has engaged fully can describe their fragrance preferences clearly and independently — without the platform's assistance. They no longer need guidance because the platform gave them knowledge that belongs to them. |

### Category 9 — Discovery Experience

**Purpose**

Measures whether the platform makes discovery rewarding, unhurried, and aligned with the guest's own curiosity — rather than herding them through a predetermined path toward purchase.

**Foundation Tracing**

Experience Blueprint (Discovery stage, The Digital Experience — Calm, Discoverable, Educational, Unhurried, Consistent), Collections and Products touchpoint.

**Evidence Sources**

- Shop search modes (empty, intent-matched, keyword)
- Intent detection and signal pills
- Dimension filters (occasion, season, character, family, vibe, projection)
- DiscoverByMoment, BestSellers, CuratedForYou, SeasonalStory
- IntelligenceSection personalisation
- Recently Viewed
- Fragrance Profile as a discovery surface
- Cross-surface continuity of discovery

**Score Guidance**

| Score | Condition |
|---|---|
| 0 | Platform is a catalogue. Guests search and filter. No discovery architecture. |
| 3 | Basic filtering and search. Intent detection absent. No personalisation. A guest's prior exploration does not influence what they see. |
| 5 | Intent detection and dimensional filtering present. Discovery feels functional and responsive. A returning guest begins to see some continuity from prior visits. |
| 7 | Intelligence-led discovery across multiple surfaces. Results feel curated, not filtered. A returning guest encounters a platform that has learned from their prior exploration. |
| 10 | Discovery adapts to the individual guest across every surface. The guest who returns encounters a platform that knows their exploration history and uses it to make new things worth discovering. Discovery is a continuous and evolving experience, not a session-level event. |

**Purpose**

Measures whether the platform treats knowledge as a gift to the guest rather than a sales instrument — building genuine fragrance literacy, not just product awareness.

## Evidence Requirements

### What Constitutes Valid Evidence

Every score assigned in a Digital Flagship Assessment must be supported by one or more of the following:

**Repository Evidence**
Specific file paths and line numbers. Component names and their behaviour. Copy quoted verbatim from the platform. Architectural patterns observed in the codebase.

**Functional Evidence**
Observed guest-facing behaviour — what a guest encounters when they visit a page, interact with an AI, or complete a flow.

**Engineering Governance Evidence**
Entries in ENGINEERING_LOG.md, SPRINT.md, CURRENT_TASK.md, or KNOWN_ISSUES.md that confirm or contradict a score's basis.

**Historical Evidence**
Prior assessment reports, for trend verification.

---

### What Is Not Permitted as Evidence

**Assumptions:** "This probably works because the architecture suggests it."

**Intent:** "The intention was to align this with the Foundation." (Intention is not implementation.)

**Prior Knowledge Without Verification:** "This was working last quarter, so it is still working." (Verify.)

**Marketing Claims:** "The homepage says it's a curated experience." (Evaluate whether the experience is actually curated, not whether the copy claims it is.)

**Analogy:** "This is similar to how [example] worked, so it likely meets the standard."

Every score is earned by evidence of current, actual implementation. Nothing less.

---

### Evidence Preservation

The assessor must record the evidence basis for each score alongside the score itself in the Assessment Report. Evidence records must be sufficient for a future assessor to verify that the score was earned — not simply assigned.

Evidence records are part of the permanent Maturity History and must not be discarded or summarised in ways that remove the ability to verify past scores.

## Improvement Planning

### From Scores to Programmes

Every assessment produces a set of scores. Scores produce priorities. Priorities produce programmes.

**Priority Order for Programme Selection:**

1. Any Tier 1 category below the current level's gate minimum — this is the first priority, always.
2. The lowest-scoring Tier 1 category — regardless of gate status.
3. Any Tier 2 category that is preventing a gate for the next level.
4. The lowest-scoring Tier 2 category.
5. Tier 3 categories, by score.

**Programme Sizing Guidance:**

A score gap of 1–2 points on a single category typically requires a focused engineering programme of 2–5 days.

A score gap of 3–4 points typically requires a programme of 1–2 weeks with multiple phases.

A score gap of 5+ points (particularly in a Tier 1 category) requires a foundational programme: inspection, planning, phased implementation, and verification over multiple sprints.

**Programmes should not attempt to close more than three categories simultaneously.** Focus produces better results than breadth. An institution that improves three categories by 2 points each achieves more institutional progress than one that touches seven categories and improves none by more than half a point.

## Quarterly Review Process

### Step 1 — Preparation (Week 1 of the quarter)

Confirm the Foundation Programme is complete and unchanged. If any Foundation document requires extension (not weakening), that extension must be completed and committed before the assessment begins.

Read .ai/ENGINEERING_LOG.md to understand what has changed since the prior assessment. Identify which categories are most likely to have shifted.

---

### Step 2 — Full Assessment (Week 1–2)

Conduct the full twenty-category assessment following the Assessment Methodology defined above. Do not skip categories because they are unlikely to have changed. Verify the current state. Trust the evidence, not the memory.

---

### Step 3 — Trend Analysis (Week 2)

Compare current scores to the prior quarter's assessment. Identify:
- Categories that improved (record the engineering programmes responsible)
- Categories that regressed (identify the cause — new feature, copy change, drift)
- Categories that are stable (verify they are stable, not merely unexamined)

Regression in a Tier 1 category is a serious finding. A programme to address it should be opened within the same quarter.

---

### Step 4 — Programme Recommendation (Week 2)

Using the Priority Order defined in Improvement Planning, identify the three to five highest-priority programmes for the coming quarter. State each programme's Foundation tracing, the category it addresses, and the score gap it aims to close.

---

### Step 5 — Report and Record (Week 2–3)

Produce the Assessment Report in the standard format. Record it in the Maturity History. Update PROJECT_STATUS.md with the current Maturity Level and Score. Update .ai/CURRENT_TASK.md and .ai/ENGINEERING_LOG.md per the standard engineering governance process.

---

## Annual Institutional Review

Once per year, the Quarterly Review is extended to include:

**Review of the Foundation Programme itself.** Have the Foundation documents remained true to the institution? Does anything require clarification or extension? (Nothing is weakened. Only additions and clarifications are considered.)

**Review of the Maturity Model itself.** Have all twenty categories remained relevant? Have the scoring descriptions remained accurate? Have new categories become necessary due to institutional expansion? The Maturity Model is designed to grow with the institution — not to be discarded and replaced.

**Trend review across the full year.** Plot Maturity Score across the four quarterly assessments. Is the institution moving toward Level 5 over time? Are any categories in persistent decline?

**Stewardship assessment.** Could a new builder arrive today, read the Foundation Programme, run the Maturity Assessment, and understand what needs to be done and why? If the answer is no, the knowledge transfer work is itself an engineering priority.

## Amendment Process

This framework may be extended as the institution grows. It may not be weakened.

**Permissible amendments:**
- Adding new categories when the institution expands into areas not covered (e.g., retail presence, international markets)
- Clarifying score guidance when assessments reveal genuine ambiguity
- Updating Evidence Sources to reflect new platform capabilities
- Adjusting tier assignments when experience demonstrates a category belongs in a different tier

**Impermissible amendments:**
- Removing categories
- Lowering score thresholds or gate requirements
- Removing the evidence requirement
- Adding exceptions to the Minimum Level Gates
- Retroactively revising the Maturity History

Every amendment must be committed with a standard engineering programme entry in ENGINEERING_LOG.md citing the Foundation Articles the amendment serves.

## Closing Principle

This framework exists for one reason: to give the institution an honest way to measure how faithfully its digital presence expresses what it believes.

The platform will drift if it is not measured. Features will be added without Foundation alignment. Copy will be written without voice discipline. Trust will be depleted in small amounts too gradual to notice without an instrument designed to notice them.

The measurement is not the goal. The goal is the institution — faithful to its purpose, honest about its gaps, and improving toward the experience its guests deserve.

Use this framework to serve that goal. Never as a performance to be optimised. Never as a number to be managed. Only as the discipline of honest self-examination that allows the institution to grow better, not merely larger.
