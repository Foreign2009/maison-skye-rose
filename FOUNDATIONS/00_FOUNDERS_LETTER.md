# A Letter From the Founder

*To Skye. To Rose. To everyone who will one day carry this forward.*

---

This company began with a compliment.

I told a colleague at work that the fragrance she was wearing was beautiful. She smiled. And then something I was not expecting happened — she stood a little taller. She carried herself differently for the rest of that exchange. A small thing had shifted. I watched it happen.

Later I asked what the fragrance was. She told me. I looked up the price.

And I asked myself a question I could not stop thinking about: why should that kind of confidence only belong to people who can afford that price?

I am not sure I have ever asked a simpler question, or one that mattered more.

---

What I had witnessed was not vanity. It was dignity. The feeling that you are well-dressed for the world, that you have taken care in how you present yourself, that you are worth the attention of a careful nose — that feeling changes how a person stands, how a person speaks, how a person enters a room. It is real. And it is available, in our industry, almost exclusively to people who can afford to pay for it at luxury prices.

I did not accept that. I still do not.

Maison Skye & Rose is not a discount operation. It is not an imitation of luxury, and it is not a compromise. It is an argument: that the confidence, the elegance, the craft, and the care that have always been associated with quality fragrance can be made accessible without making them lesser. That accessibility is not the enemy of quality. That you do not have to choose between the two.

That argument has been the business from the beginning. Everything we have built — every product, every piece of the platform, every decision about how to treat a customer — has been built to make that argument true in practice, not just in promise.

---

Your names are on everything we build.

Skye. Rose.

I want you to understand what that actually means. Not in the emotional sense — you will understand that easily enough — but in the practical sense. It means that every product we release, every customer we serve, every promise we make, and every corner we consider cutting is done under your names. If we are careless, your names are on it. If we exaggerate to make a sale, your names are on it. If we treat a customer as someone who only matters until a more profitable customer comes along, your names are on it.

I put your names on this institution deliberately. That weight is not decorative. It is a mechanism for integrity — a way of ensuring that the people who run this company, now and in the future, feel the full consequence of how they behave.

Your names are not a brand. They are a standard.

---

I have thought a great deal about what it means to build something that outlasts the person who built it.

It requires a different kind of discipline than building something successful. A successful business can be built through ambition and intelligence and a measure of luck. A lasting institution requires something harder: the willingness to make decisions whose benefits you may never see, for the sake of customers you may never meet, sustained by values that feel inconvenient precisely when they are most necessary.

I am trying to build the second kind.

That means we grow with the customers who believed in us before we were proven, not away from them. It means that as we expand into fragrances, body care, home fragrance, and whatever comes after that, the same standard of care follows into every category. It means that every employee who joins this institution is joining something that has a particular way of behaving, and that way of behaving does not change when it becomes costly to maintain it.

Growth is not the goal. Building something worth inheriting is the goal. Growth, if it comes from the right foundations, follows from that.

---

The hardest thing I know to build in this work is trust.

Trust takes longer than revenue. It is more fragile than a supply chain. It cannot be manufactured through marketing. It can only be accumulated through behaviour — by not lying when it would be convenient to exaggerate, by not exploiting when it would be easy to, by not abandoning when it would be profitable to move on.

A customer who trusts us will come back. They will tell someone. They will give us the benefit of the doubt when something goes wrong, because they have learned that something going wrong is not who we are. That kind of relationship is worth more than any short-term conversion, any growth metric, any quarterly number.

Trust is also what this institution's name will eventually rest on. I want these names to mean something that has nothing to do with how the products smell. I want them to mean: this company told us the truth. This company cared about the quality of what they sent us. This company did not stop caring about us once they had our money.

That is the reputation worth building. Everything else is easier to buy.

---

If you are reading this as someone who has joined Maison Skye & Rose as an employee or steward: welcome. You carry these names now too.

I am not asking you to be perfect. I am asking you to be honest, to care about the quality of your work, and to treat every person you serve with the dignity they deserve. The customer who spends the smallest amount with us deserves the same care as the customer who spends the largest. If that ever stops feeling true inside this institution, something important will have been lost.

The products we make should be worth being proud of. The promises we make should be the kind we intend to keep. The company we are building should be one that a reasonable person, learning everything about how it operates, would feel good about trusting.

Hold that standard. It is harder than it sounds. Hold it anyway.

---

Dear Skye. Dear Rose.

When you are old enough to understand what this is, I hope you understand why I named it after you and not after myself.

I named it after you because you are the reason that long-term thinking matters to me. Because it is easier to make a good decision when I am thinking about what this institution will look like when you inherit it. Because your names carry something mine does not: the future.

I do not know what shape this company will take over the coming years. I do not know which products will find their way into which homes, or how many customers will carry this name into parts of the world I have never visited. I do not know what the industry will look like when you are old enough to understand it.

What I know is that I wanted to build something honest. Something made with care. Something that tells the truth about what it is and delivers on what it promises. Something that believes people deserve access to quality regardless of what they can afford, and proves that belief through what it actually does.

That is the institution I have tried to build. Whatever it becomes, I hope it remains that.

I love you both more than any of this.

Dad

---

*Written at the founding of Maison Skye & Rose.*
